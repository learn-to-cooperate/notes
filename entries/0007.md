# The shadow side of "cooperation"

**Date:** 2026-02-04


## Learning to cooperate (notes toward something real)

Cooperation is usually sold as warm consensus, shared purpose, the comforting rhythm of “we.” Yet the moment phrases like “together we are stronger” (or “one,” “better,” “unstoppable”) appear—whether chanted in meetings, printed on posters, or embedded in mission statements—something subtle shifts. The word “we” begins to feel less like an open hand reaching out and more like a line quietly being drawn in the dark. What presents itself as an appeal to belonging starts to function as a sorting mechanism: who is folded in, who is left standing just outside the frame.

## The unease beneath the chant

This sorting is rarely announced. It happens in the background, almost politely. The ones who do not fit—the overlooked contributors, the quietly sidelined voices, the people archived in the algorithmic oubliettes of .gitignore, or those already carried away into silence—are not loudly rejected. They are simply not included in the picture the chant is painting. Their absence becomes the invisible foundation that lets the collective image hold together. True cooperation, if it is ever to mean more than a slogan, has to learn how to notice this sleight of hand: to feel the hollowness it leaves behind and to refuse to let the easy rhythm of “we” drown out the question of who was never counted.

In the communal sphere, the performative utterance of "together we are stronger" stirs a quiet unease. It poses as a Hegelian Geist of organizational unity, yet the "we" is not a phenomenological horizon of intersubjective Mitsein but a panoptic mechanism of inclusionary violence—relegating the unassimilated, those cast into liminal zones of disregard, the algorithmic oubliettes of .gitignore, or the thanatological void of the deceased—to the apophatic underside of discourse, where their non-being silently props up the illusory plenitude of the collective. True cooperation learns to feel this hollowness, refuses the easy chant, and insists instead on remembering who was never invited to the "together." This leaves the fragile edifice of purported synergy feeling hollow and precarious—yet perhaps that's exactly where real learning to cooperate begins.
